# Instagram reach without followers: a month-1 follow-up for a solo practitioner

## Snapshot
- **Business type:** one-person practice offering emotional guidance, online consultations and workshops
- **Size:** single professional, small established audience
- **Region:** the Americas
- **Engagement:** free pilot: report 1, a 15-minute month-1 call, report 2
- **Tools:** public-profile scrape, the client's own Instagram Insights screenshots, a recorded call with permission, AI assistants (Claude)
- **Status:** report 2 delivered; no outcome claimed (see update log)

## Challenge
After report 1 the owner changed a lot at once and published far more than before. The question at month 1: what did she apply, what moved, and what should the next 30 days focus on, with a small audience, little time, and several launches planned.

## Approach
1. **Baseline vs now.** The same public metrics as report 1, rescraped: format mix, calls to action, hashtags, comments per post.
2. **Month-1 call (15 minutes).** What she applied and what she skipped, what was most useful, what is coming in her business, and an honest question about whether she would pay for the report.
3. **Her own Insights.** Screenshots of the last 30 days: views, accounts reached, followers, content type, audience.
4. **Report 2** in plain Spanish, four pages: what changed, applied or not, what worked, at most three adjustments, a four-week checklist, tools, and how the next report will measure progress.

## What we found
1. **Our own tooling was wrong about the account type.** The scrape flagged the account as personal; her dashboard showed a professional account. We had recommended a switch she did not need. Always verify the account type in the client's own app.
2. **Reach grew about 3.5 times in 30 days, but net followers stayed flat.** People saw the content, and nothing invited them to take a next step. For a practice with an event coming, the missing piece was a sign-up path, not more posts.
3. **Volume decayed.** Daily views peaked within a few days of a posting burst and then fell, even though posting continued. What was posted that day matters more than how often.
4. **Format split.** Reels reached noticeably more non-followers than feed posts and stories, which were seen mostly by existing followers (read from the Insights bars, so approximate).
5. **The audience fits the offer.** Close to nine in ten followers are women and roughly three in four are aged 35 to 64, the same group the next event targets.

## Decisions and trade-offs
Three adjustments, in this order:
1. **Cut to two or three posts a week.** The client herself named the burnout risk, and views fell while volume stayed high.
2. **Make sign-ups for one free event the month's goal,** with a keyword comment ("comment X and I send the link") and a second link in the bio. This turns existing reach into a measurable action.
3. **Use reels to reach new people and stories for existing followers.**

The alternative was to keep the high cadence and test what sticks. It was not chosen: the data showed decay, and the owner's energy was the scarcer resource. It would win if sign-ups showed that volume, not the lack of an offer, was the limit.

## Deliverables (described, not shared)
- Baseline vs now table with the three metrics that were unavailable marked "no data"
- Four-page report 2 for the owner, in Spanish
- A message to send the report and request the missing screenshots
- Notes on what the client said a paid version should include

## Status
Report 2 is delivered. No growth or revenue result is claimed. The client has agreed to share the three missing Insights screenshots; those and the results of the four-week plan will be added in the update log.

## Lessons
- Verify account type and settings in the client's own app before recommending anything.
- Ask for Insights screenshots at report 1, so month 1 has a real baseline.
- When reach rises but followers do not, give people a next step.
- A client who is energized by report 1 may over-publish: plan the cadence, not just the content.
- Ask the pricing question directly and expect a polite answer; weigh it against paying clients later.
- A client asked for tools and resources in plain language, not only a to-do list: add a fixed section to every report.

## What I would do differently
Request the client's Insights at the first report and check the account type with her before the first audit.
